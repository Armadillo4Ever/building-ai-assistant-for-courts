# Building an artificial intelligence assistant for a court: architecture, safeguards, measurement. Lessons from two applications developed by a judge

**Author: Armadillo4Ever.** Licence: CC BY 4.0. Date: 23 September 2026.

*Version 1.1, aligned with version 1.12 (18 September 2026) of the internal technical note from which it derives; updated with it.*

*English version of article_fr.md; the French text prevails.*

## Abstract

This article reports, in the form of a methodical account of experience, on the construction of two applications for analysis and drafting support intended for a French administrative court: a litigation application, the workstation of the rapporteur judge, and a demonstrator restricted to one form of mass litigation. Developed by a judge working as sole developer, they share a hybrid search engine (vector and lexical, reciprocal rank fusion, optional reranking by a cross-encoder), national and European corpora split into passages, and a large language model called through a programming interface under strict citation rules. The article describes the architecture, the framework of technical safeguards and human control, then the evaluation method: a benchmark of 287 questions drawn from the official case abstracts of the case law, a hand-built benchmark of twenty real questions, and a benchmark of litigation case files whose outcome is known, with an automatic judge calibrated on a sample reviewed by a human. The results are given as they are, gains and failures included, and the methodological findings are formulated so as to be reusable.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models have, within a few years, given rise to an abundant supply of legal assistants. The literature shows at the same time that these tools, including the best-resourced commercial products, cite non-existent or inaccurate sources at rates that rule out use without verification (Dahl et al., 2024; Magesh et al., 2025). The question facing a court is therefore not whether a model can draft a text that resembles a decision, but how to build a system in which a judge keeps the initiative and the decision, in which every statement is tied to a verifiable source, and whose performance, series after series, can be measured in terms of what it sees and what it misses.

The setting of the case study is that of the French administrative courts (administrative tribunals (*tribunaux administratifs*), administrative courts of appeal (*cours administratives d'appel*), cassation court). Before these courts, a rapporteur judge receives the case file, investigates it, and drafts an analysis note and a draft decision which are then deliberated collegially. The application described here is a workstation for this rapporteur judge, to which is added a demonstrator restricted to one form of mass litigation, that of paid parking.

This article is not an independent evaluation. It is written by the author of the applications, a judge who alone designed, coded and measured them, with the assistance of a language model for development and writing. The benchmarks were built by him, the automatic judge that scores the results is the model itself, and the human calibration rests on a sample of fifteen lines. These limits are set out in section 8 and should be borne in mind with every figure. What the article claims to offer is something else: a description of the architecture, the safeguards and the measurement method precise enough for a reader to reuse its elements, and an account of what was tried, what worked and what failed, with the figures in each case.

The article is aligned with an internal technical note, kept up to date from version to version, whose figures it reproduces without modification; it is revised with it.

## 2. Two applications, one common base

### 2.1 Functions

The litigation application is the workstation of the rapporteur judge. It receives the case file as lodged, reads it, extracts from it the exhibits (*pièces*), the claims (*conclusions*), the pleas (*moyens*) and the preliminary questions, launches on its own the first searches for sources of law, then drafts, on the judge's instructions, the analysis note, the draft decision or the screening order, inserting the sources found. Each user has a workspace in which the files of the case are lodged (from the electronic filing platform for applications, or scanned). The pleas, the claims and the preliminary questions are each identified with their page and an exact quotation from the written pleading, which the judge checks and validates before any drafting. For each plea, the engine queries the corpora and returns verified sources with their link. A dialogue in natural language with the case file or with the note is possible, the engine answering by citing the exhibit and the page. The documents produced are the analysis note, the recitals (*visas*), the draft decision and the draft orders.

The restricted demonstrator covers paid parking litigation. It produces a draft decision and a report from a case file, on a lexical corpus of 1,249 passages, with its own pipeline but the method of the litigation application.

The two applications share the same search engine and the same corpora, described below as the base of the litigation application.

### 2.2 Models used

The guiding principle is that everything that can run locally runs locally: encoding, lexical search, reranking and text recognition let no data out. Only generation goes through an external provider (table 1).

| Role | Model | Where it runs | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Encoding of passages and questions (vectors of dimension 1,024) | bge-m3 (BAAI, MIT licence; Chen et al., 2024), served by Ollama | locally (development workstation, test server) | no data leaves for vector search |
| Lexical channel | BM25 on SQLite FTS5, own normalisation (accents, plurals, stop words, article tokens of the type "L. 511-1") | locally | index of 79 MB for 281,788 passages; built in 22 s |
| Reranking of candidates (second stage) | bge-reranker-v2-m3 (BAAI, Apache 2.0), cross-encoder | development workstation (graphics accelerator, fp16, 108 ms per pair); disabled on the test server, without a graphics card | without a graphics card, no variant proved useful (section 6.2) |
| Generation (note, draft, order, splitting of case files, query rewriting, description of image pages, dialogue) | Claude (Anthropic), model claude-sonnet-5, 1M tokens of context, structured JSON output, prompt cache | at the provider (United States), over HTTPS | only external component |
| Text recognition of scanned case files | tesseract (French) on the test server; the system's recognition engine on the development workstation | locally | four pages in parallel; re-read rotated by 90°, 180° and 270° when a page yields no legible word |

Table 1. Models used.

BM25 remains a strong baseline in case-law retrieval (Rosa et al., 2021), and cross-encoder reranking follows the principle described by Sun et al. (2023).

### 2.3 The corpora

Each corpus is a pair of files: a passage file (one passage per line, with its metadata, its reference and its link) and a vector file. The corpora are updated by scheduled tasks; the global search queries fifteen of them, the others answer dedicated buttons. They are all built from public French and European sources, which matters for the reproducibility of the approach: administrative case law (decisions of the court abstracted in the Recueil Lebon, level A, 87,252 passages, and mentioned in the Tables, level B, 194,536 passages, extracted from ArianeWeb; 40,397 analysis abstracts; 248,828 passages from the conclusions of the public rapporteurs (*rapporteurs publics*); classification scheme; the old Recueil Lebon OCRed from Gallica, a pilot of 1,356 decisions); Légifrance held locally from the DILA open data dump (159,892 articles of codes and 210,049 articles of Acts, ordinances and decrees in force; the PISTE API remains in use for consultation at a given date, named articles and history); Constitutional Council (*Conseil constitutionnel*) (7,268 decisions and the commentaries in the Cahiers); Court of Cassation (*Cour de cassation*) (144,000 judgments published in the Bulletin, merged at query time with Judilibre); European Union law (judgments, opinions and preliminary references from EUR-Lex and Cellar; full-text corpus of legislation in force, 43,029 acts, under construction); European Convention on Human Rights (the Convention and the official guides by article, 2,191 passages, judgments cited in the guides from HUDOC, "guides first" search); treaties and agreements of France; personal data (the 1978 Act, the GDPR, case law of the CJEU, deliberations of the CNIL, opinions of the EDPS); internal drafting guides; and lastly form corpora, drawn from the templates in the open data of the administrative courts, distilled into form only and never used as search corpora, which prevents the reasoning of another case from being reused as if it were a source. The total represents 11 GB.

### 2.4 The processes

**Reading a case file.** The exhibits lodged are stitched into a single bundle whose bookmarks are preserved. The pages are OCRed page by page, with a cache; pages without legible text are described by the model (photograph, blank page, handwriting, form, plan) and filed as supporting exhibits. Without bookmarks, the file is read in full (ceiling of 900 pages) then split into exhibits according to the text, by the model. The engine then identifies the nature of the litigation, the subject matter, the object, the claims of each party, the preliminary questions raised, the texts relied on and the pleas, each with its page and an exact quotation from the written pleading. The judge's manual corrections prevail and are preserved.

**Search.** A question is encoded; for deep search, it is first rewritten by the model in the vocabulary of the decisions (sentences, notions, texts), the query vector being the centroid of the question and its rewritings (Wang, Yang and Wei, Query2doc, 2023; Zhou et al., 2023). The first stage gathers, for each corpus, the candidates from the vector channel and those from the lexical channel, merged by reciprocal rank fusion (RRF, k = 60; Cormack, Clarke and Büttcher, 2009; on hybrid search in non-English law, Louis, van Dijck and Spanakis, 2025). On the development workstation, a second stage reranks up to 100 candidates with a cross-encoder; on the test server, without a graphics card, this second stage is disabled. The "quick" search returns three to six sources per corpus; the "deep" search, which corresponds to the lawyer's reasoning from a body of converging authorities, returns up to fifty sources per corpus. A "classified case law" mode browses the classification scheme without a semantic engine. The state of the art in case-law retrieval is surveyed by Feng, Li and Ng (2024).

**Generation.** The analysis note is produced in structured sections (presentation, claims, preliminary questions, one object per plea with statement and reasoned solution, proposal) from the sources assembled by corpus, the exhibits of the case file and, since the mid-September 2026 version, the text of the written pleadings and of the contested decision (up to 100,000 characters, placed at the head of the prompt with cache). The draft decision is drafted only after the judge has fixed the direction of the decision and, if he so wishes, the answer to each plea, the outline and the texts to be relied on; his instructions prevail over the note. Screening orders are drafted from a catalogue of types and from the case file, without a note. Each document produced is reviewed by form and citation checks, then edited in tracked changes by the judge, who validates it. These choices rest on the work on drafting supported by retrieved sources (Magesh et al., 2025; Kim and Lee, 2025; Hou et al., 2024) and on the effect of position in a long context (Liu et al., 2023).

**Observed costs and times.** On the development machine, 1,485 calls to the model were recorded between mid-August and mid-September 2026 for a total cost of EUR 41 (of which EUR 6.4 for notes, EUR 1.4 for the HyDE trial, EUR 1 for image description). On a case file of ordinary size, reading costs EUR 0.1 to 0.4, the note EUR 0.2 to 0.5, an image page description a few thousandths; the generation benchmark of twenty case files cost EUR 13. The whole exploration represents in the order of EUR 800 as at mid-September 2026. A note is drafted in one to two minutes, and a scanned case file of 75 pages is read in eight minutes on the test server. The operating cost of such a system is therefore not the main item; building the corpora and the measurement is.

### 2.5 Software architecture

The litigation application is an HTTP server written with the Python standard library, without a framework; the demonstrator is a FastAPI application. The interfaces are single HTML pages, with no build step and no client-side dependency apart from a PDF reader. The corpora are read by relative path in a flat working directory, which makes the package portable. The prerequisites are modest: Python 3.12, an encoding server with bge-m3, tesseract with the French package, 32 GB of memory, a few tens of gigabytes for the corpora, and a network route out to the interface of the generation model. Development and measurement are carried out on a workstation equipped with a graphics accelerator (engine, corpora, benchmarks, encoding server, reranker) and on a test server distinct from the development workstation, without a graphics card, where the reranker is disabled.

The hard point is the generation model. All drafting, case-file splitting, rewriting and dialogue tasks currently go through an American provider, under a contract that excludes training on the data but retains inputs and outputs for up to thirty days (section 3.4). An institutional use presupposes a sovereign model, along three possible routes, each to be measured with the generation benchmark: an open-weights model run on internal infrastructure (Mistral, Llama, Qwen or equivalent, generally one server-class graphics card per instance, quantisation possible); a hosted offering qualified under the national security standard for cloud computing, exposing a compatible interface; and, as a transitional measure, the current provider with zero data retention. The engine isolates the calls to the model in a single function, which limits the replacement work, but three capabilities must be verified in the replacement: a context window of at least 128,000 tokens (the text of the exhibits and the sources of a note commonly occupy 40,000 to 80,000), reliable JSON output with automatic repair, and, for image pages, visual reading or a fallback to text recognition alone. The prompt cache, which divides by ten the cost of exhibits re-read from one call to the next, is a convenience, not a necessity.

## 3. A constitution for the systems and technical safeguards

### 3.1 The framework

The two applications are governed by a single document, called the "constitution for the systems", whose version 0.2 (25 pages, September 2026) is integrated into each of them and published under a CC BY 4.0 licence for the text and MIT for the accompanying code. It lays down 29 articles in six titles, 16 technical safeguards (G1 to G16) with, for each, its nature, its function and the check that verifies it, a base of core instructions in eight points, and a code annex (safeguard functions, tests, adversarial tests). It refers to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on artificial intelligence (Annex III, point 8(a): systems intended to assist a judicial authority, classed as high-risk; Articles 4, 14, 26 and 27), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026 which postpones the application of Annex III to 2 December 2027, to the 2018 European ethical charter of the CEPEJ, to the Council of Europe Framework Convention on artificial intelligence (CETS 225) and to Article L. 10 of the Code of Administrative Justice. The institution is never named in it, any more than in the code: the framework is written for a court, not for this one.

The rules that protect are not all of the same order: constraints placed on the model in its instructions, deterministic checks on output, interface arrangements that require an action from the user. The document distinguishes these natures and states, for each safeguard, what verifies it. Safeguards planned but not yet in place are named as such: tagging of exhibits in the prompt (G2), automatic citation check by re-reading (G4), flag for instruction injection in exhibits (G8), origin notice on every document (G14), common base of instructions placed at the head of all calls (G16), all to be measured before switching on.

### 3.2 Safeguards in place

The safeguards in place fall into three families.

The first concern citation and naming. The model cites only sources appearing in the list supplied, reproduces their reference and their link exactly, and grounds every statement on the verbatim or the abstract supplied; links are verified, decisions carry their level (A, B, C) and there is never a bare URL. An exhibit is named by its real designation, never an invented one; a page that nothing identifies is called "unidentified document", never "illegible". No matching by rank is done with the model: pages, images and articles are labelled and copied back, answers outside the batch discarded; this rule stems from a silent failure observed in August 2026, in which the model returned descriptions of scanned pages in an order that was not that of the pages sent. Lastly, no em dash appears in a generated document, by a deterministic filter on the output of every generation and by a punctuation rule in the instructions, in both applications.

The second concern control of the outcome and the integrity of documents. No draft is written without the direction fixed by the judge; the note is generated only after validation of the list of pleas and claims, and any later change to that list calls it into question. A generated document is a document of the case file: note, draft or order are filed in the case workspace as soon as the task finishes and redisplayed on opening without relaunching generation; this safeguard stems from an incident in which a note produced and delivered to the browser disappeared on page reload, because the result existed only in the page that had requested it. Likewise, each search made with a case file open is kept server-side with its question, its corpora, its mode, its date and its results. The engine does not rewrite in tracked changes what nobody has modified: word-by-word tracking serves only to preserve what the judge himself changed. The example values that contaminated searches when a case file was open have been removed; a screen that has nothing to show says so.

The third concern transparency of operation and operational safety. The server verifies that the code executed is that on disk. The software sets HTTP security headers, in both applications. A common banner says what the engine is doing in real time: current step, steps done, elapsed time, then "Finished" or the failure; the steps are real when the server reports them, and the rare steps still estimated from the usual duration are marked as such. At analysis, a rule without any call to the model reads in the first two pages of the application which court it is addressed to; if it is not the workspace chosen, the discrepancy is stated, quoted with its page, and the judge can switch with one click or keep the workspace; nothing is switched without him. A bundle none of whose exhibits bears a stamp is left in the order in which its author assembled it. Lastly, form checks review the documents produced against the internal drafting guides, with six checks on orders (legal basis, recitals, hearing formula, operative part).

### 3.3 Human control, in the interface

The judge sees and corrects the list of exhibits, of pleas (with page and quotation) and of claims before any drafting; he chooses the nature of the litigation, the subject matter and the axis of search, inferred from the case file but modifiable; he fixes the direction, the answer to each plea, the outline; he converses with the engine about the note without that dialogue drafting anything; every document produced is edited in tracked changes and validated; a permanent warning recalls that the system can be wrong and that verification rests entirely with him; a reporting button and a search log exist. Orders are drafted only on a click, on a chosen type, with the legal basis displayed. Several adjustments came from trials with volunteer judges: validation of the pleas is confirmed by a message; search results carry the colour of their corpus.

**What the tool is, and is not.** The aim is not a "judge model" that would decide the dispute, even if the studies on that subject serve the evaluation benchmark; it is an aid to analysis and drafting, in which the judge keeps the initiative and the decision. This distinction dictates the form of the documents produced.

**Present first, propose at the end.** The rule, fixed by the author and applicable to the analysis note, is that the document does not present itself as a fully drafted text following the solution envisaged by the engine. For each plea or point of law, it first gives an objective presentation of the sources, not drafted, in bullet points: one or two sentences of synthesis on the direction in which the sources point, then the sources themselves with the verbatim of the important passages, and the identification of what points the other way. Only at the end comes a proposal from the engine, identified as such. Not drafting keeps the reader's attention; separating the analysis from the sources lets him form his own opinion; the final proposal, set apart, leaves him some distance from the engine's analysis.

This form matches what research on assisted decision-making has established. Over-reliance on the machine's suggestion is the main risk of decision-support tools; explanations added to a conclusion do not reduce it by themselves and may worsen it (Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos, 2021; systematic review of 35 studies by Romeo et al., 2025). What reduces it: arrangements that force cognitive engagement, such as delaying or separating the recommendation, and a low cost of verification, that is, sources immediately readable (Vasconcelos et al., 2023; de Jong et al., 2025); explanations by examples help better than abstract explanations (Chen et al., 2023). In a controlled experiment with 206 lawyers, machine highlighting of the relevant passages reduced task time by 30% without loss of quality, where drafted summaries did not bring that gain (Nielsen et al., 2024). The implementation of this form, called J0 in what follows, and its measurement are reported in section 5.2.

### 3.4 Data, contracts, compliance

Vector and lexical search, encoding and text recognition run locally; only the generation calls (text of exhibits, sources, instructions) go to the model provider. The provider undertakes contractually not to train its models on the data of the programming interface (commercial terms, data processing agreement with standard contractual clauses), but retains inputs and outputs for up to thirty days before deletion, except for reports (up to two years) and legal obligations; zero retention is available on request for the model used, and the decision to request it remains to be taken. This is the main limit of the experiment, and the reason why an institutional use would require a sovereign model or a qualified offering.

The demonstrator permanently displays the notice "insert only non-confidential or anonymised data"; a reversible pseudonymisation tool, run on the workstation, exists for case files containing personal data; the test case files are fictitious or old. Terms of use (data controller, retention periods for case files and logs) and a record of processing activities (five activities, processors) have been drafted; the impact assessment remains to be produced, as does a box for accepting the terms. The software imposes named access, restricted to an allow-list of institutional domains, with password and two-factor authentication by email; there is no public presentation page; secrets appear in no repository and the code repositories remain private, apart from the constitution and the generic tools. The code is under the EUPL 1.2 licence; the encoding and reranking models under MIT and Apache 2.0 licences; the corpora come from public sources.

The technical documentation provided for by Annex IV of the European regulation on artificial intelligence has been drafted and filed in the compliance file. It sets out, point by point, what the Annex requires, and names three structural gaps rather than passing over them: the defence against instructions slipped into exhibits is not in place, the actual use of human control is not measured, and the system depends on a model that is not European.

## 4. Evaluation method

The method common to all the benchmarks is the following: each benchmark is a frozen file of questions or case files with their expected results; every measurement goes through the same script, on the same benchmark, and compares two configurations; the figures serve to compare, not to establish an absolute quality, because the relevance judgements are incomplete by construction. The results are recorded with their timestamp and their configuration. No trial was retained in the current version without prior measurement by the application's ordinary path, and the rule has been to keep nothing that does not gain measurably, and to record the failures with their explanation.

### 4.1 Abstract benchmark, drawn from the case-law analyses

The abstract benchmark draws its questions from the official abstracting (abstracts of the classification scheme) of the court's decisions, stratified by level and by decade; the target is the abstracted decision. Version 1 had 300 questions (August 2026); version 2 has 287, after removal of 13 "questions" that were merely paths in the classification scheme. The measures are nDCG@10 (quality of the top of the list; Järvelin and Kekäläinen, 2002), recall@50, the number of questions with no expected source at all, and the number of seconds per question. The construction draws on COLIEE (2025) and LegalBench-RAG (Pipitone and Houir Alami, 2024); the use of model-generated relevance judgements is discussed by Ma et al. (2024). This benchmark has a known caveat: its questions are written in the very terms of the abstracts, which by construction favours a lexical channel. That is why the fusion weight retained was set from the hand-built benchmark, not from this one.

### 4.2 Hand-built benchmark and external benchmarks

The hand-built benchmark gathers twenty real legal research questions, put by judges, with their redacted answers (authorisation obtained; 236 sources, of which 79 load-bearing), never published. It measures recall, because the lawyer's search aims at exhaustiveness and reasons from a body of converging authorities. It is the only benchmark on which a fair comparison with public engines can be made, since its questions appear in no indexed summary. Two public engines were in fact measured on abstract benchmark v1: JusticeLibre (0.530 nDCG@10 with the raw question, 0.694 with the salient words) and LibreJustice (0.364). These figures are not comparable with those of the application: JusticeLibre indexes the Légifrance page of the decision, abstract summary included, where the benchmark question appears word for word, which was verified.

### 4.3 Litigation case-file benchmark

The generation benchmark was built in mid-September 2026 from some thirty administrative litigation case files in varied subject matters (tax, immigration law, liability, planning, interim proceedings), of sizes ranging from a few pages to several thousand, including ten case files from the litigation paper of the direct recruitment competition for administrative judges (2017 to 2026), which are public practice files. The ground truth is, where public, the corresponding decision in full text, or the marking guidance published in the examiners' reports. No case file is described or identified, and none leaves the development machine.

For each case file: reading and note by the application's ordinary path, then an abstract of the decision (direction, preliminary questions, claims, pleas and answers, texts) and a matching, both established by the model, which is therefore the judge. The literature shows that such a judge is lenient, sensitive to position and length, and must be calibrated on a sample reviewed by a human (Thakur et al., 2024; Jain et al., 2025; Norman et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2025); in law, scoring by data points (LeMAJ, Enguehard et al., 2025) and binary "all or nothing" criteria (Legal Agent Benchmark, Harvey, 2026) served as models. The measurement grid has one line per plea, per question and per text of the decision, filled in twice by the automatic judge, the second time in reverse order, with a minority veto on the line criteria and calculation of Cohen's kappa between the passes. It distinguishes "seen in the list" submitted to the judge from "dealt with in the note", failing which a plea that the judge himself set aside would count as an omission by the engine. The direction of the operative part is reported separately, with a three-state status (concordant, split, divergent), independently of the grounds.

The measurement principle, fixed by the author, dictates the hierarchy of indicators: the outcome of a dispute is the result of collegial deliberation; being wrong on the outcome is not serious, not having seen the questions is. The main measure is therefore the coverage of the pleas and questions and the sourced and verifiable character of the analysis; the concordant direction remains displayed in second rank.

### 4.4 Human calibration

Until mid-September 2026 the automatic judge was calibrated against itself: its two passes agreed, without anything saying whether they agreed with a judge. The author therefore scored fifteen lines himself, on three case files, chosen where the automatic judge hesitated most, answering the same two questions as it: had the engine seen this point, and is what it writes about it verifiable. The calibration pages now also carry six overall criteria scored from 1 to 4 (completeness, logic, accuracy, quality of language, operational usefulness, authority of sources), adapted from a grid published for a Chinese legal assistant (ChatLaw, 2023); they will serve to compare two versions of the engine other than by counting.

### 4.5 Mechanical verification of citations

A last instrument measures not the quality of a note as a whole but the truth of one precise element: the passage quoted from an exhibit. Having the model scored by the model would have cost one call per case file and proved nothing. The recognised text of the exhibits is on disk: a quoted passage is found there or is not. The verification compares the passage with the text of the page cited, after normalisation, by exact containment then by the best sliding window, and returns four states: established; wrong page when the passage is in the case file but elsewhere; not found; outside the recognised text when the page exceeds what recognition produced, a case excluded from the rate. The note is never rewritten: each source receives its verdict. The procedure is free, instantaneous and replayable on notes already produced; it belongs to the line of chain-of-verification (Dhuliawala et al., 2023), but without a model.

Two remedies add up. Reindexing repairs the old decades: decisions in capitals, until then reduced to a single passage by a case-sensitive splitter, are now split correctly, and the share of decisions from the 1960s found rises from 11% to 74%. Reranking improves the top of the list everywhere. The lexical channel is the strongest on this benchmark because the questions are written in the very terms of the abstracts; this caveat is noted, and the fusion weight was set from the hand-built benchmark. On the test server, first-stage recall (0.843 at twenty results returned) is lower than on the development workstation (0.906): a difference of index or of lexical channel to be examined. In total, the share of expected decisions found rose from 50% (August 2026) to 91% at the first stage of the search.

**Hand-built benchmark.** On the administrative case-law corpus, the number of expected decisions found, out of 96, evolved as follows: 9 in August; 12 with selection by relevance (instead of sorting by level then cutting); 18 with query rewriting; 32 in deep search (100 candidates reranked, 50 returned); with lexical fusion, recall rises from 0.35 to 0.50 on the nine questions with targets in the corpus. On Légifrance: 1 article found out of 70 through the API, 9 with rewriting, 17 with the local corpus, 19 with both. The local corpus built from the open data dump is, on this benchmark, the clearest gain, at 1.2 seconds per question.

Before and after handing the text of the written pleadings to the drafter (three case files): concordant direction 1 out of 3 then 3 out of 3; answers to pleas 4 out of 11 then 10 out of 12; the cost per case file rises from EUR 0.08 to 0.10. The note quite simply did not have the facts. One measure was corrected on that occasion. The grid had at first returned "divergent direction" on all twenty-one case files, including where the decision and the note both say dismissal: the question put to the judge was ambiguous, one pass answering on the operative part and the other on the path followed, and the minority veto is unsuited to an overall assessment. The question now bears on the direction of the operative part; the direction for this series is not usable. The note and the judge having changed on the same day, the gap from the first series cannot be attributed to one rather than the other: this series stands as a starting level, at constant instrument.

**The form of the note, measured by a control series.** A series then set against each other two arms identical save for the guard, on nineteen case files: form J0, which presents before proposing, against the old form. Preliminary questions seen rise from 28 to 44%, without loss on the pleas. The new arm at first seemed to pay for this gain with forty-one lines dealt with in excess; examination of these lines showed that the grid counted as surplus the questions that the note expressly declares moot, which is the opposite of a defect. The grid now distinguishes them: the real surplus falls from forty-four to five for the new arm, against three for the old. The form is retained in the mid-September 2026 version.

### 5.3 Generation: the three causes of insufficient coverage, dealt with one by one

The three avenues designated by the J0 + J1 series were applied and measured each behind its own switch, on the same benchmark and in "note only" mode, so that the only thing that changes is the modification measured. A measurement counts as a measurement, not as a demonstration: three of them returned a negative result, reported here on the same footing as the others.

**A defect of the drafter found by measurement.** The drafter's output ceiling was set at 12,000 tokens; on case files where the list of pleas grows long, the note reached this ceiling and came back empty or truncated, without anything signalling it. The ceiling was raised to 48,000 tokens, and form J0 holds at the scale of twenty-three pleas.

**J2, pleading abstracts, and J4, the consolidated list of pleas.** J2 has each written pleading read in full, in labelled slices of forty pages, and reduces it to a structured abstract (claims, pleas and limbs, facts, exhibits relied on, dates, procedural questions, each with its page), in the spirit of the work on reading long documents in slices (Zhang et al., 2024; Laban et al., 2024). J4 consolidates in one call the pleas identified in these abstracts and those from the first automatic reading, and the consolidated list replaces the one the note must deal with. The measurement covered the three largest tax case files, whose decisions together distinguish thirty-four pleas (table 5).

Raw consolidation went down to the level of the argument and returned fifty-seven pleas; tuned to the level that the judge distinguishes, with a bound of twenty-five, it returns twenty-three and raises coverage from seven to sixteen out of eighteen. The price is clear on the other two case files: the longer the list, the more the note is spread thin, judged sourcing falling from 0.80 to 0.66 and elements in excess rising from eleven to forty-one.

A design trade-off was drawn from this by the author. The two errors are not of equal weight: a plea the engine has not seen is invisible to the judge, whereas a plea too many is visible and costs him one click. The engine therefore casts wide, and the consolidated list goes up into the screen where the judge checks and validates the pleas before the note is launched. The cost of the spreading was paid on the note, hence after validation: tightening upstream removes it where it arises. Each line of the list carries its limb, its page and the sentence of the written pleading from which it is drawn, without which sorting twenty-five lines would be endured rather than done.

The two mechanisms do not do the same thing and, badly placed, they work against each other. The own-motion examination carries the gain in question coverage and, poured into the note, competes with the pleas for a bounded space: on one case file, the note deals with a single plea and five preliminary questions. The subject-matter grid brings up no question but holds the note together. Placed together, the two made the drafter lose the output schema on two case files. The cause was a location: the grid was at the very end of the user instruction, just after the list of pleas to deal with, hence at the place where the drafter reads what it must return. Moved into the method instruction, with a reminder that it does not change the output schema, it no longer breaks anything. The own-motion survey, now separate from the note, was compared directly with the questions that the decision distinguishes: the union of what the judge has before his eyes, note and survey, covers eleven questions out of nineteen fully, fifteen counting partial coverage, against six in the reference series. Question coverage doubles at no cost to the note. What the survey misses is a matter of the particular case rather than of the subject matter (prior amicable settlement procedure, sufficient link between decisions contested in a collective application, representation by counsel, no need to adjudicate): so many lines to add to the closed list.

**A note off schema, and the safeguard drawn from it.** Three times in the same series, the drafter returned a perfectly valid JSON object that was not a note; automatic repair, which repairs malformed JSON and not off-topic JSON, found nothing to object to, and the interface displayed an empty note without saying why. The output is now checked on its form, a single retry is made with an imperative reminder of the schema, and if the second fails, the failure is named. The split measurement served from its first series: on the case file whose note was lost, the grid writes "pleas seen 9 out of 10, in the list 9, in the note 0"; without the distinction, that case file would have been counted as nine omissions by the engine, when it was the drafting that had failed. One finding remains open: on seven case files of the seventeen, the note cites no exhibit of the case file with its page and sources itself only on the corpora.

### 5.4 Verification of citations and human calibration

**J7, the quoted passage opens with one click.** In the note, a source drawn from the case file was displayed as dead text while a source from the corpora carried its link; the judge had to find the page by hand to verify a quotation, that is, in practice, not verify it. The reference and the passage are now clickable, the exhibit opening at the page with the passage highlighted, without any call to the model, in line with the result of Nielsen et al. (2024).

**J6, what the first verification found.** On twenty-one notes, one hundred and fifty-three passages drawn from the case file were compared with the text of the exhibits: eighty-eight exact, eighteen not found, and forty-four exact but attached to a page other than the one indicated. The cause was not an invention by the model but a design defect: the text of the exhibits was handed to it in one piece, without any page marker. It was asked to ground each fact on a page without ever being given the pages; it estimated them. The pleading abstracts, for their part, label each page and have the label copied back. The correction consisted in doing the same, at no cost. The stakes had changed in nature with J7: since the page cited is a link, a wrong reference sends the judge to the wrong place, which is worse than no link.

| Verification of passages drawn from the case file (17 case files) | Before labelling | After labelling |
|---|---|---|
| Passages verified | 144 | 103 |
| Exact at the page cited | 80 | 78 |
| Exact, but at another page | 44 | 5 |
| Not found in the case file | 17 | 18 |

The count of wrong pages admits of no ambiguity, forty-four against five; the overall rate must be read with caution, two case files having changed configuration between the two measurements. What the correction does not repair is more interesting: the passages not found remain at the same number, nearly one in five. These are not reference errors but near-quotations, tightened or slightly reworded, presented in quotation marks as if copied exactly, their similarity with the real text lying between 0.4 and 0.6. That is precisely what the study by Magesh et al. (2025) notes about commercial legal tools, and it is the kind of inaccuracy that a hurried re-reading does not see. The verdict of the verification is therefore displayed next to each source in the note.

**Human calibration, first lesson: on the instrument.** Agreement between the author and the automatic judge, on the fifteen lines scored, stands at 0.714 on coverage, for a kappa of 0.456, and at 0.857 on verifiability, for a kappa of 0.689. The second is acceptable, the first is not: a kappa of 0.46 is moderate agreement, and it forbids taking the coverage figures as established without re-reading. The sample is small, which also forbids drawing a fine correction from it. The discrepancies go both ways: on one case file the judge treats as "subsumed" what the human treats as not seen, on another it treats as not seen a plea that the human recognises.

**Second lesson, which did not concern measurement.** In the margin of one line, the author noted that it was odd that the engine did not have a given article of the General Tax Code. The article was indeed in the local corpus: it was not absent, it was unfindable. An article number in Latin ordinal form looks, to an encoding model, like all its neighbours, so that similarity search returned the neighbouring articles and never the one requested. An article named in the question is now read directly in the corpus, by its number and its code, and placed at the head. The defect applied to a notable share of the General Tax Code and the Book of Tax Procedures.

**Third lesson, drawn from verification.** Of the two hundred and fifty-three citations of exhibits found in the notes of the benchmark, two hundred and forty-seven carried a verbatim, or 98%, but only one hundred and fifty carried a page, or 59%. The note thus cited the case file without saying where, and two mechanisms were paralysed by it: the link that opens the exhibit at the right page did not form, and the verification had nothing to compare. Sourcing seemed secured; it was only half so. The schema now requires that every citation of an exhibit carry its page, read from the page label and not estimated. On the three case files most affected, the proportion of citations with their page rises from 0 to 95%, and the verification now establishes twenty-eight passages out of thirty-nine there.

The whole is integrated into the mid-September 2026 version, with citation verification enabled. The judge sees, next to each quoted passage from an exhibit, a notice "passage verified", "wrong page" followed by the real page, or "passage not found in the case file", and the link takes him to the real page.

### 5.5 Reading of case files and form of documents

**Reading.** On a flat scanned case file of 41 pages, the mid-September version recognises six exact exhibits, the nature cassation and the applicant, with no page declared illegible, where the previous version confused half of them; the main cause was a race between execution threads on the temporary file of text recognition, which returned identical pages.

**Costs and expenses, or the fourth proof of the same rule.** Article L. 761-1 of the Code of Administrative Justice, on costs not included in the expenses, was the text most often missed: present in the list of claims fourteen times out of nineteen, dealt with in the note only five times, although the instruction required it. The cause was not the instruction but the output schema, which reserved no place for costs: what has no box is not written. A dedicated heading was therefore opened in the schema, verified by the mechanical citation check like the other blocks.

**Two defects found on a case file of different origin.** A competition paper, a fictitious case file of thirty-seven scanned pages without a list of exhibits, passed through the ingestion chain outside the benchmark, revealed two defects that the test case files, all supplied with a schedule of exhibits, could not reveal. The first: the exhibit count was read from the schedule alone, so that the banner announced "Exhibits (0)" in front of eleven exhibits identified by the splitting of the text. The second is more serious: the filing of an exhibit in the folder of the earlier instance rested, in the absence of a meaningful file name, on the court mentioned in its text; correct on appeal and in cassation, the rule filed, before an administrative tribunal, in an imaginary first-instance folder the decisions of the cassation court lodged as documentation, that is three exhibits out of eleven. The rule no longer applies when the court seised is an administrative tribunal, the provenance of the file remaining sovereign. Both corrections were measured, with their counter-check on appeal.

**An end-to-end check.** Sixteen steps were replayed route by route on a test server distinct from the development workstation, on a parallel instance of the code, with a competition case file: lodging, analysis (216 seconds, 59 pages), note (164 seconds, six pleas out of six, 14 sources established out of 21), draft, order, dialogue, searches, export, working state. Sixteen passes, EUR 1.83, no error trace. One serious anomaly was nevertheless noted: the analysis having identified no preliminary question, the note was silent on admissibility, when the real decision decided four such questions. The answer: when there is no question at all, the admissibility grid (discontinuance, jurisdiction, no need to adjudicate, time limit, standing and interest, prior administrative decision, representation, claims) opens, and only then; it can cost nothing to questions that do not exist. Real trial on the same case file: eight questions dealt with, including the four of the decision, the direction unchanged.

**Economy of calls to the model.** Four corrections, in order of gravity. First a failure: the model client was built without timeout or retry, and the benchmark stopped indefinitely; a timeout of six hundred seconds and two retries were set. Then a local repair of truncated JSON, attempted before the repair call to the model. Then the prompt cache, decided call by call: on the drafting of the note, called once per case file, writing the cache is a dead loss, billed a quarter more than ordinary input; on the tasks where the same base is re-read from one call to the next, a single reuse pays back the write. Lastly, routing by task to a light model for tasks that do not require the reasoning of the main model. One idea was discarded: the output token ceiling is not a cost lever, since one pays for the output produced and not for the ceiling.

### 5.7 The legal basis as the reference point of the search

On the benchmark question "does an administrative authority endowed with a power of sanction constitute a tribunal within the meaning of Article 6(1) of the European Convention on Human Rights?", the deep search returned judgments from the Recueil Lebon of 1850 and 1856. The author first framed this as a defect of temporal filtering, then, more accurately: when the question carries the mention, direct or indirect, of a legal basis, the engine must have that legal basis as the fundamental reference point for contextualising the other terms. The engine gave equal importance to "sanction", "administrative authority" and "Article 6(1)"; hence results that a lawyer regards as absurd. In the mean vector formed from the question and its rewritings, the legal basis was treated only as one word among others, and the reranker, for its part, does not know that the Convention dates from 1950.

Four steps were introduced, all inactive when no legal basis is identified. One: the translation asks the model to identify the legal basis, direct or indirect ("fair trial" refers to the same one), with its date of entry into force, that of its applicability in France if different, the forms in which decisions cite it, and one sentence of context; in case of doubt about the dates, nothing: better nothing than a wrong date. Two: the legal basis counts double in the mean vector, the context one and a half times. Three: the reranker receives the question preceded by the legal basis and the context. Four: a decision earlier than the legal basis cannot apply it; candidates dated before its entry into force are discarded, except for a question of legal history, and those that cite it move to the front; the step says so ("25 results earlier than 1974 discarded: they cannot apply the legal basis").

Measurement, on a test server distinct from the development workstation, same question, same rewriting, same corpora, the anchoring removed then restored. Administrative case law: before, one hundred results of which twenty-five earlier than 1974 and seventeen earlier than 1950, the oldest from 1850; after, seventy-five results, none earlier than 1974, the oldest from 1986, nineteen expressly citing Article 6 and placed at the head, the first being CE, 27 April 1994, no. 127115. European Convention corpus: one entry dated 1828 before, none after, forty-four out of fifty-six citing the basis. The legal basis identified by the model: Article 6, paragraph 1, of the Convention, in force on 3 September 1953, applicable in France since 3 May 1974. Cost: the translation already existed (one call, cached); the rest is local. A measurement on one question is not a benchmark: the rule will be re-measured on the hand-built benchmark once its questions have been revised with their legal bases.

Two things were found while measuring. The model's answer, longer since the addition of the legal basis, exceeded the nine-hundred-token ceiling of the translation; the cut-off JSON could no longer be read, and the rewriting came back empty without saying so, which also deprived the search of the sentences and notions; the ceiling was raised to one thousand eight hundred. And on the test server, the second reranking model is not installed, so that the deep search there returns the hundred candidates in order of similarity, without reranking, whereas the page texts told the user that the candidates were "re-read one by one by a second model"; the texts were brought into line with what happens.

### 6.3 Methodological findings

The following findings emerged over the course of the series. They are formulated so as to be reusable outside the case study.

**A benchmark can lie.** The first measurement of the light reranker had run without a reranker, the model download having failed silently; a "register of abstracts" measurement that keeps the abstracts of the targets is trivially perfect; a public engine that indexes the abstract summary finds the question word for word. Every protocol is therefore checked by a control, that is, a reference configuration re-measured the same day. Likewise, a silent failure is found only by a benchmark that counts what ought to be written: an empty note returned in the form of a valid JSON object had triggered no error (section 5.3).

**Gains do not simply add up.** The reranker gains 0.11 on vector alone but 0.04 on lexical fusion; hence the order of the following avenues. Cost is measured call by call, measurement and benchmark data do not leave the machine, and switch-over decisions are taken on figures.

**An instruction with no reserved place in the output schema is not followed.** The finding was verified four times, on unrelated subjects, including the grounds for cassation and costs and expenses: the instruction was written, clear, and the output bore no trace of it, for want of a box in which to write it. Opening the box suffices; reinforcing the instruction never suffices. Corollary: a method instruction goes in the system instruction, never at the end of the user instruction, where it is treated as a remark. But an output box is not free: the series in section 5.6 show that two boxes added, one per plea and one per admissibility question, raised nothing and accompanied a fall in concordant direction from 6 out of 10 to 3 out of 10. The hypothesis to retain is that the box shifts attention towards filling it in. The rule therefore becomes: open a box for what must be said and is not said, one at a time, and measure it, concordant direction included.

**Rule of symmetry.** A defect found somewhere is looked for everywhere the same code, the same rule or the same action recurs; and conversely, a remedy found somewhere is looked for in all the other places. The occasion was the throttle on the parallelism of character recognition, present from the start in the demonstrator, absent from the litigation application, where its absence cost 18.5 seconds per page against 0.31.

**The weak link is not always the one you think.** Before fine-tuning an embedding model, measure search alone, without a model, against an independent truth: if corpus coverage explains most of the shortfall, fine-tuning would change nothing. Likewise, a truncated answer is not a short answer: every call whose output is structured reads its stop reason and says so, and a ceiling is set with a margin, never to the bare minimum. And an interface text is a statement: it says what the machine on which it is displayed does, not what the development workstation does.

**The lawyer has a hierarchy that the cosine does not.** A legal basis is not one word of the question among others: it is the frame within which the others are read. Giving it that rank (identification, weight, head of the reranker input, temporal bound) made the absurd results disappear without inventing any; the rule holds for any semantic search in law.

**A homogeneous test set measures only what it contains.** Two ingestion defects escaped nineteen test case files because all carried a schedule of exhibits and came from the same mould; a case file of different origin showed them in one run. Lastly, a change to the code shortly before a trial session with judges is the risk of showing a broken application; what must be shown is prepared the day before with what the application already keeps.

## 7. Open avenues

The generation avenues numbered J0 to J7 have, for the most part, been carried out and measured. J0 (form of the note) is done. J1 (overhauled measurement: binary grid per case file drawn from the decision, double judge, order neutralised) is done, but human calibration remains to be extended. J2 (pleading abstracts and citations to the page), J3 (own-motion examination and subject-matter grids, with a safeguard for cases that are not matters of public policy and an own-motion survey kept separate from the note), J4 (one criticism equals one plea, at the level the judge distinguishes, list bounded at twenty-five, in the line of the work on legal argument mining by Habernal et al., 2022, Al Zubaer et al., 2023 and Deng et al., 2024), J6 (mechanical verification) and J7 (highlighting in the exhibits) are done and measured. J5 (the judge's office (*office du juge*) according to the level of jurisdiction: detection of the level and of the nature of the action, own-motion investigation in cassation, on appeal and in full-jurisdiction proceedings (*plein contentieux*), on the model of the legal syllogism studied by Jiang and Yang, 2023, and Song et al., 2025) remains to be done. On the interface side, the own-motion survey is written to disk but not yet displayed to the judge, where it must take the form of a distinct, collapsible block, like the engine's proposal.

Next in high priority come: the preliminary questions of the court's own motion (time limit and lateness from the dates of notification and registration, standing and interest to act, prior administrative decision, joinder), which call for a dedicated ingestion step; very large case files, where the written pleadings exceed the limit on the texts transmitted (30,000 characters per exhibit, 100,000 in total) and where, beyond 900 pages, the bundle is no longer read in full; the grouping of pleas; the coverage of the decision corpora, which requires widening the corpus to recent decisions, published or not, measuring pure search, without a model, against an independent truth, and only then returning to the reranker and then to fine-tuning; uncodified texts; the sovereign model, with the generation benchmark to measure the loss; the re-measurement of the legal basis rule on the hand-built benchmark, with several bases per question and dates of the norms verified against Légifrance rather than stated by the model; and compliance (data protection impact assessment, fundamental rights impact assessment provided for by Article 27 of the regulation on artificial intelligence, zero retention at the provider, box for accepting the terms), before any widening.

In medium priority: fine-tuning the reranker on the 40,397 analysis abstracts (benchmark targets excluded), then its quantisation, the only measured route to a useful second stage on CPU; safeguards G2, G4, G8, G14 and G16, with adversarial tests before switching on; whether or not to install the reranker on a server without a graphics card; re-matching the benchmark. In low priority: the avenues from the literature review (listwise reranking by the model, sparse mode of bge-m3, graph of cross-references between decisions, extraction of the paragraph of principle, late chunking after Günther et al., 2024, fine-tuning of bge-m3), each to be measured on the benchmark, in order; and the idea that the engine asks a question when the search is ambiguous, one question at most, never a systematic dialogue.

## 8. Limits of this account of experience

The limits of this work are of several orders, and they condition the reading of every figure.

The first concerns the author. The designer, the developer, the builder of the benchmarks, the human calibrator and the writer of this article are one and the same person. There has been no independent evaluation, and the relevance judgements, incomplete by construction, were established by the person who had an interest in the system working. The figures compare configurations with one another; they do not establish an absolute quality.

The second concerns the automatic judge. The generation benchmark is scored by the model that drafts, and the literature shows that such a judge is lenient, sensitive to position and length. Human calibration covers fifteen lines and gives a kappa of 0.456 on coverage, which is moderate agreement and forbids taking the coverage figures as established without re-reading. The concordant direction of the second series is not usable, its measure having been corrected after the event.

The third concerns the benchmarks themselves. The abstract benchmark favours the lexical channel by construction, since its questions are written in the terms of the abstracts. The hand-built benchmark has twenty questions. The generation benchmark has some twenty case files, of which three incomplete and three mismatched in the first series; on ten case files, a gap of two pleas or two questions is within the noise. The legal basis rule was measured on a single question. The test set was homogeneous, and two ingestion defects were seen only on a case file of different origin.

The fourth concerns scope. The applications have been tried by a few volunteer judges; the actual use of human control is not measured, and nothing yet says how a judge uses, in his work, the list of pleas, the own-motion survey or the verification verdicts. The system depends on an external, non-European generation model, through which the data pass with retention of up to thirty days; that is the main limit of the experiment, and what an institutional use should lift first, with any loss of quality measured by the benchmark described here.

The fifth concerns the form of this article, aligned with an internal technical note whose passages too closely tied to the institution or to persons have been omitted rather than paraphrased.
